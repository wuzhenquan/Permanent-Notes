**The Flashcards in Eudic**

I’ve been practicing with Eudic flashcards daily to reinforce my vocabulary, and what I’ve found most valuable are the ten example sentences for each word. They give me plenty of opportunities not just to learn new words, but also to practice them in real contexts. Most sentences are challenging, drawn from TV shows, speeches, news articles, and novels. I try not only to understand every meaning but also to read them aloud with the audio, which helps make the words feel more alive. 
          
The spaced repetition system really helped. Repeatedly going through the words let me review the difficult sentences and better remember the different contexts [[in which]] each word is used.
          
I also really appreciate that Eudic keeps the source sentence of each new word in its listening practice app. Being able to recall where I first encountered a word adds an extra [[layer]] to the memory—it’s like meeting the word for the first time again.
          
Looking back, I can see real progress. My vocabulary feels more solid, I’m learning more new words from each example sentence, and my reading has definitely improved. Using Eudic Flashcards has become more than just a study habit—it’s a way for me to engage deeply with English every day.

**Philosophy of Capturing Ideas in Roam**

When an idea came to mind, I wrote [[a few important words]] in brackets. Those brackets then became a gateway back to that idea. 

This approach is quite basic—I had known about it for years—but I didn't realize that the simplest method is often the best way to gather ideas. All brackets are your ideas. 

Using this approach, my mind became clearer, and I began to see how ideas interacted in the Graph Overview. I also realized that the main content of a page doesn’t always need to live in long blocks of text. ^^A page’s content can be distributed rather than centralized^^—what’s essential may exist (not in the page itself, but) in backlinks from [[Daily Notes]].
